Resume and CV photo guide

How to make a professional photo for your resume or job application

Start with a clear photograph of yourself, choose a presentation that fits the application, and review the result carefully. This guide explains the practical choices behind a useful professional headshot.

A resume photo, CV photo and job application portrait often serve the same purpose: helping a reader recognize you. A profile photo may appear as a small circle, while an application form may ask for a rectangular image. Begin with the destination’s instructions rather than assuming every application wants the same picture.

Create your professional photo

First, check whether a photo is requested

Photo expectations differ between employers, applications and destinations. Use a photo when the application requests or allows one, and follow any instructions about size, background, file format and editing. If a resume does not need a photograph, you can still prepare a separate portrait for a professional profile where appropriate.

Take a better starting photograph

  • Face the camera in even light. Keep both eyes visible and avoid strong shadows across your face.
  • Use a sharp original image with space above your hair and around your shoulders. A tiny face in a large group photograph gives the editor less useful detail.
  • Keep the camera roughly at eye level. Avoid an extreme overhead or low-angle view.
  • Keep hands, accessories and other objects away from the face. Use a recent image that represents how you currently look.
  • Upload the original file when possible. Screenshots and repeatedly compressed copies can lose detail.

Choose an outfit and background

Use a business outfit or a simple shirt that suits the role and the application. A clean neckline and a quiet background help the face remain the main focus. IDProm offers outfit and background choices in its English editor; you can inspect the preview before unlocking a download.

A plain light background is easy to place on many application forms, but the requested background takes priority. Check the edges around hair, shoulders and clothing, especially when choosing a background that contrasts strongly with the original photograph.

Review an AI professional headshot before using it

AI portraits can change details. Compare the preview with your original: check facial features, skin detail, hair, clothing edges and the shape of your shoulders. Choose a result that still looks like you. If something appears distorted or artificial, adjust the portrait or use a better starting photograph before downloading.

For a professional profile, test the image in a small square or circular crop. For an application form, keep the crop within the requested dimensions without cutting off hair or the chin. Studio provides additional positioning and editing controls.

Download a file that fits the application

After unlocking your photo, choose the available PNG or printable PDF delivery option. Use a digital image for an online form when it accepts that format; use a PDF when you need a print layout. Check the destination’s accepted file types and file-size limit before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

Can I create a resume photo from a selfie?

A clear, evenly lit selfie can be a starting image. Avoid strong perspective distortion, clipped hair, blur and heavy filters. Review the generated portrait against the original before using it.

Is IDProm only for Thai users?

No. The English photo editor is available in a browser and includes international size labels. Follow your employer’s or institution’s requirements for the finished image.

Can I use the same AI headshot for a passport or visa?

Do not assume that a professional portrait meets official-document rules. An issuing authority may require an unedited photograph and prohibit AI changes. For example, review the U.S. Department of State’s passport photo guidance. A suitable crop or file size alone is not proof of acceptance.
