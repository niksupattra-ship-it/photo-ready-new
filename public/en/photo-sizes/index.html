Photo sizes in millimetres, inches and pixels

A physical print size and a digital pixel size describe different things. Use this table to understand the English editor’s presets and choose the format your application actually requests.

IDProm’s English editor includes original size, 35 × 45 mm, 2 × 2 inches, 33 × 48 mm and 50 × 70 mm. The preset PNG crop uses 300 pixels per inch. Original size keeps the original image dimensions instead of applying one of these physical-size crops.

Millimetres, inches and pixels: what changes?

Millimetres and inches specify a physical size on paper. Pixels specify the number of image samples in a digital file. One inch equals 25.4 mm, so a 2 × 2 inch square is 50.8 × 50.8 mm. It is not exactly a 51 × 51 mm square.

To calculate a dimension at 300 DPI, divide its size in millimetres by 25.4, multiply by 300 and round to the nearest whole pixel. For example, 35 mm ÷ 25.4 × 300 is approximately 413 pixels. The same calculation gives approximately 531 pixels for 45 mm.

Choose a crop without stretching the face

Select the requested preset in the size controls, then adjust the photo’s position and zoom in the preview. Changing the crop should change the visible framing, not stretch the face to fit a new rectangle. Leave the necessary space around the head and keep important details inside the frame.

A square profile picture and a tall application photograph need different framing. When moving from one shape to another, review what is removed at the edges. If the source is too tightly cropped, use a photograph with more space rather than enlarging a tiny part of the face.

Digital submission or printed photo?

For an online application, use the requested image format, pixel dimensions and file-size limit. A physical-size preset does not automatically satisfy a website that asks for a particular pixel range or another file format.

For printing, unlock your photo and select the available printable PDF option. Use the correct paper size and print at actual size or 100% when you need the layout’s physical dimensions. A printer’s “fit to page” option can change the finished size. Check the printed result before cutting or submitting it.

Dimensions are only one part of official requirements

Passport, visa and other official applications can also specify head size, eye position, background, expression, recency and restrictions on editing. IDProm’s AI portrait tools can change an image, so do not assume a portrait is suitable merely because its dimensions match a listed preset.

Check the issuing authority’s current instructions. The U.S. passport photo guidance, for example, calls for an original, unedited photograph. Use the required photographic process for the destination instead of relying on a preset’s name.

Questions about photo size

Does 35 × 45 mm mean 350 × 450 pixels?

No. The physical dimensions and pixel dimensions are separate. At 300 DPI, a 35 × 45 mm crop rounds to 413 × 531 pixels.

Does a larger pixel count guarantee a sharper photo?

No. Enlarging a low-detail or blurred source does not restore information that was not present. Begin with a clear original image and inspect the result at the size you plan to use.

Which size should I choose for a resume photo?

Follow the employer’s application instructions. If no physical print size is specified, choose a crop that fits the resume layout or profile field. The resume and job application photo guide explains the starting-photo and framing choices.
